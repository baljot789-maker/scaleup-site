Architecture

Build the root before the branches

What a build looks like once it's more than one automation. These are categories, not results. The case studies are where the numbers live.

Go to the numbers

The principle

Most teams buy twelve tools and get twelve different voices. Every person solves style, tone, and brand terms separately inside their own process, and within a quarter the output has drifted in a dozen directions. The fix is structural and unglamorous: the rules live in one place, and everything downstream reads from it. Update the root once and every branch inherits the change. Get this backwards and scale makes the problem worse instead of better.

Root layer Style, brand terms, positioning, personas

Sits once. Nothing below it duplicates it. Its savings never appear as their own line item because they are already inside every number the branches produce.

Branch 1: Publishing operations

Drafting to house style, two-pass editing, one asset rewritten for four channels. Hangs directly off the root, which is why output stays consistent across whoever happens to be writing.

Settles: "Everything needs a cleanup pass from the one person with taste."

Branch 2: Outreach and media

Pitch drafting, per-contact personalization, background prep before a call. Same root, so the pitch sounds like the company rather than like a template.

Settles: "Personalization is the first thing we drop when we're busy."

Branch 3: Executive visibility

Starts as individual voice profiles for two or three leaders. Then the branch splits: instead of building a profile for every new person, you build the thing that makes profiles. Someone hands over two or three writing samples and gets a working voice back. That is how a program goes from three executives to fourteen without adding fourteen builds. The generator is the branch. The people are the leaves.

Settles: "Leadership wants to post but it dies after two weeks."

Branch 4: Pre-flight review

A scan for claims, absolutes, uncited stats, third-party names, and anything that triggers a legal round trip. Runs before submission rather than after rejection, which is what removes the second review cycle.

Settles: "Compliance is where our timeline goes to die."

Branch 5: Reading your own numbers

Skeptical review of plans, dashboards, surveys, and personas before they get committed to. Catches the double-barreled survey question before you collect 400 useless responses. Status gets assembled from what actually shipped instead of reconstructed from memory.

Settles: "We report on activity because we can't get to outcomes."

Which branch you need is what the audit answers

Nobody builds five. The audit ranks them against your actual week, and the root gets built once underneath whichever one you start with. That's why the second build costs less than the first.
