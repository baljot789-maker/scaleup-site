Earlier builds

Four systems, and what changed after each one

Systems Joe built in previous roles, anonymized to keep former employers and colleagues out of it. Two carry measured before-and-after numbers. Two are estimated from what the work took, and say so.

Whose work this is.

These come from roles Joe held, so they belong to those employers rather than to ScaleUp. They're here because they show what the same approach produced when someone let him build it.

02Measured

A four-person team took on 15 more clients on the same headcount

A content agency of four was hand-writing and hand-tuning every client blog and release, so capacity was capped at whatever four people could physically produce. Growth meant hiring, and hiring meant margin.

Joe built a per-client voice profile system and a drafting workflow with keyword and pillar-page mapping wired in, then handed it to three writers. About nine in ten drafts came back in the client's voice ready to go. The remaining one in ten needed a person, which is why a person stayed in the loop.

10 clients carried, up from 10, on the same four people
10 clients25 clients

The system won zero clients. It's the reason winning them cost zero salaries.

03Measured

A launch week that ate 25 hours now takes 10

An in-house communications function ran on one person's memory. Every launch got rebuilt from scratch and quality depended on who happened to be doing it.

Joe documented the actual working style first, then built the drafting workflow around it, with pitch generation, per-outlet personalization and one standard reporting format. Same output, same standards, measured against his own before and after as the person who owned the function.

25 hours per launch week, down from 25
0 hrs25 hrs

The larger result is that it stopped being his. Anyone on the team could run a launch afterwards.

04Estimated

Ten people stopped re-solving the same problem every week

A marketing team of about ten was writing to a style guide that had gone missing years earlier, so every piece got an informal cleanup pass from whoever cared most. The same corrections happened over and over in a dozen separate places.

Joe built one shared style and brand-terms layer that every downstream workflow reads from, so the rules live in a single place. Update it once and everything inherits the change.

0 estimated hours a week, across ten people
estimated~2,400 hrs a year

Estimated from what the work took at about five hours per person per week, rather than clocked.

05Estimated

Eight people stopped writing every creative brief from scratch

Briefs were rebuilt from a blank page every time, so two people briefing the same kind of work produced two different documents. The gaps surfaced later, when the deliverable came back wrong and everyone had a different memory of what was asked for.

Joe built a brief generator that reads the company's own corpus and brand guidelines, then produces the brief and the templates that go with it. Anything complicated escalates on its own to a short meeting with the people who own the final deliverable.

0 estimated hours a week, at 4 per person across eight people
estimated~1,664 hrs a year

Briefing turned from a writing task into a review task. The argument now happens over a draft that already exists, which is a much faster argument to have.

Every one of these started with somebody writing down how the work actually gets done, then building on top of that. The Plan is that written-down part, sold on its own. See what gets built from it.
