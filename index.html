Sidekick Crew is $4,500 a month against that $7,790. That's 42% less, which is why we say about 40% and leave “half” to everyone else.

Why we say 40 where others say 60. Pick base salary as your baseline and this number reads 14 percent. Pick a flattering market and you can push it past 60. Loaded cost is the only baseline that matches what actually leaves your budget, because payroll taxes, health coverage, paid leave, retirement and supplemental pay all land there the moment someone starts. So that is the one we publish, and it's the one to hold anyone else to.

Rebuilding the multiplier from its components lands at 1.478 against the top-down 1.4598, which agree within 1.2%. Components: payroll taxes 8.1%, health insurance at $7,885 employer share for single coverage (KFF 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey), paid leave 13.7%, retirement 5.4%, and supplemental pay 6.7%.

It adds to them. This is capacity your team directs, and your team gets the credit for what ships. The people you have stay on the work only they can do, which is the part that gets them promoted.

What if the work is bad?

You see a Sample first, so you judge the work before any money moves. After that we review everything against the brief before it reaches you, and a month that ships under your agreed number is a month you keep your money, so bad work costs us rather than you.

Am I locked in?

Crew runs month to month, and cancelling means telling us to stop. Read the fine print elsewhere in this category, where month to month on the pricing page turns into a ninety-day commitment in the FAQ.
