What each one does

Hours are from the sample audit, not a client result. Yours land wherever your own week says they land.

The copywriter

Takes one approved idea and writes every version of it your channels need, in the voice your firm already uses.

Writes: blog posts, social series, email sequences, landing pages, and the fourth rewrite of the thing you already wrote three times.

9.5hrs a week, from rewriting one asset for four channels

The analyst

Pulls the numbers out of every tool and assembles them into the format your leadership already reads.

Builds: the monthly report, campaign wrap-ups, and the slide someone always asks for the night before the meeting.

7hrs a week, from pulling numbers by hand

The researcher

Reads what your customers and your market actually said, then routes what matters to whoever owns the answer.

Produces: sourced findings memos, competitor reads, and customer feedback sorted by theme instead of by whoever got to it first.

6hrs a week, from sorting feedback by hand

The ad creator

Turns one concept into a set of testable variants that still sound like your firm rather than like an ad library.

Produces: headline and body variants, channel-sized cuts, and the volume you need for a test to actually mean something.

Varieswith how much you run

Your own, whatever it is

Every firm has one job nobody else has. The odd report, the strange approval chain, the thing your industry demands and no software covers.

Built to order: after the audit ranks it against everything else, so you only build it if it earns the time it takes.

The strategist

This one is different, and it should stay that way. Strategy is the work your people were hired for, and it is the last thing you want handed to a machine.

What changes: the sample audit found four hours a week going to campaign planning. The agent does the reading and the first pass so those four hours become real thinking time instead of catch-up.

4 → morehrs a week, protected rather than replaced

30.5hours a week

The copywriter, the analyst, and the researcher account for 22.5 hours a week in the sample audit. The rest is the compliance drag: chasing review cycles cost that team another 8 hours, and it falls because every agent reads your rules before writing rather than after a reviewer catches it.

That is 30.5 hours a week, the same figure the time map reaches from the other end. At a team of six it is close to a full salary you are already spending.
